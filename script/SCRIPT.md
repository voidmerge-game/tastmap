# The Unification of Italy, 1815–1871

## Scene 1 — A peninsula divided
### S1_01
After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna redrew Italy in 1815. The peninsula was not one nation: Sardinia-Piedmont ruled from Turin; Austria held Lombardy and Venetia; dukes governed Parma, Modena, and Tuscany; the pope ruled central states; and the Bourbon kingdom controlled Naples and Sicily.
Visual: globe to Italy; show 1815 polities; camera on Turin, Milan, Florence, Rome, Naples.

### S1_02
Revolution came in 1848, when uprisings challenged Austrian power in Milan and Venice. Defeat followed, but the idea of national unity survived. Piedmont's King Victor Emmanuel the Second kept a constitutional state; his minister, Camillo Cavour, built railways, strengthened the army, and sought allies abroad to weaken Austria.
Visual: Milan and Venice pulses; show the 1815 borders and Sardinia-Piedmont flag.

## Scene 2 — War, diplomacy, and the Thousand
### S2_01
In 1859, Cavour secured French support against Austria. At Magenta and Solferino, allied armies forced Austria to surrender Lombardy, while Venetia remained under Habsburg rule. Central Italian states voted to join Piedmont. The map changed through both battle and diplomacy, yet the northern kingdom still faced a divided peninsula.
Visual: reveal Lombardy from Solferino; callout at Solferino; archival Cavour portrait.

### S2_02
In May 1860, Giuseppe Garibaldi sailed with roughly one thousand volunteers from Quarto. They landed at Marsala, won support across Sicily, and crossed to Naples. His southern campaign was dramatic, but unity required compromise: Garibaldi handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel, whose forces had advanced through the Papal States.
Visual: route Quarto–Marsala–Palermo–Naples; flag drops; archival Garibaldi portrait.

## Scene 3 — The kingdom and Rome
### S3_01
In March 1861, the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed, with Victor Emmanuel the Second as king. Massimo d'Azeglio captured the unfinished task: We have made Italy; now we must make Italians. Venetia joined in 1866 after war with Austria, but Rome remained outside the kingdom, protected by French troops.
Visual: merge the 1861 kingdom; show d'Azeglio quote card before the next paragraph; archival Victor Emmanuel II portrait.

### S3_02
In September 1870, Italian troops entered Rome through the breach at Porta Pia after France withdrew its garrison. Rome became the capital in 1871, completing political unification, not resolving every regional divide. The borders now formed one kingdom, while language, loyalties, and daily life still differed across the peninsula.
